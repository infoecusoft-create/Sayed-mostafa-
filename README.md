Research & Development Background

The research and development trajectory of Dr. Eng. Sayed Mostafa reflects a multidisciplinary progression across electrical engineering, industrial control, embedded systems, computer vision, automotive technologies, software engineering, and artificial intelligence. Rather than emerging from a single technological domain, this trajectory evolved through successive engineering projects in which practical system requirements led to the development of increasingly advanced computational and analytical capabilities.

1. Industrial Control and Medium-Voltage Electrical Systems

The early stage of this trajectory began in 2015 with the development of a Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system for monitoring and controlling medium-voltage electrical networks.

The system addressed operational requirements associated with 11 kV and 22 kV distribution networks, including medium-voltage distribution equipment and associated monitoring and control operations. This work established an early foundation in real-world engineering systems, where software was required to interact with physical infrastructure, operational states, measurements, and control mechanisms.

This stage was particularly significant in establishing an engineering approach based on the integration of hardware, software, real-time information, system monitoring, and operational decision support.

2. Embedded Systems, IoT, and Computer Vision

Subsequent development expanded toward embedded systems and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies. A range of experimental and applied systems were developed using platforms including Arduino, ESP-based devices, and Raspberry Pi, providing practical experience in sensor integration, device-level computation, communication, automation, and distributed system behavior.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, this work extended into computer-vision-based applications. Among the developed systems was a Face Mask Detection system designed to automatically identify whether individuals were wearing protective face masks.

This period contributed to the development of practical experience in combining computational algorithms with physical devices and real-world environmental conditions, forming an important bridge between conventional engineering automation and intelligent computational systems.

3. Establishment of ECU Soft and Automotive Software Engineering

In 2025, Dr. Eng. Sayed Mostafa established ECU Soft, initiating a dedicated development environment focused on automotive software and Electronic Control Unit (ECU) technologies.

The development program included the design and implementation of specialized software for analyzing and processing automotive ECU data and binary files. Early projects included TMerge and Toyo Limiter, followed by a growing collection of specialized engineering tools.

The underlying development philosophy was not limited to producing isolated software utilities. Instead, the objective was to investigate methods for developing independent analytical and processing capabilities that could operate with reduced dependence on externally maintained databases.

This direction introduced a broader research question:

«How can engineering software infer, analyze, and process complex technical structures through computational analysis rather than relying primarily on predefined databases?»

This question subsequently became an important conceptual foundation for the development of Watcher Eye.

4. ECU Forg and the Expansion of the Development Program

In 2026, an important stage in the development of ECU Soft began with the integration of an additional key developer into the team, who contributed the ECU Forg program.

The introduction of ECU Forg represented a significant expansion of the software-development program within ECU Soft and accelerated research into the development of specialized systems for ECU file analysis, processing, and modification.

Following this stage, several additional tools and projects were developed, including:

- Smart Checksum
- BIN Vie
- Map Forg
- PIN Code
- VCM Limiter, subsequently evolved into IVECO Suite Pro

Collectively, these systems represented an incremental engineering process in which knowledge obtained from one development stage informed subsequent tools and algorithms.

The central objective was to progressively move from manually defined operations toward software capable of identifying structures, recognizing patterns, and applying engineering transformations based on the characteristics of the analyzed data.

5. From Specialized Engineering Tools to an AI Engineering Architecture

The development of these systems ultimately provided the practical and conceptual foundation for Watcher Eye.

Watcher Eye did not originate as an isolated artificial-intelligence experiment. Its development emerged from accumulated engineering experience involving binary analysis, ECU structures, pattern recognition, software development, system diagnostics, embedded technologies, and computational problem solving.

The initial objective was to develop an intelligent system capable of assisting with ECU file analysis and engineering operations. As development progressed, its capabilities expanded toward code generation, software development assistance, terminal and command-line interaction, and increasingly complex computational tasks.

The scope subsequently expanded beyond automotive applications toward interaction with networks, servers, computing environments, system operations, and engineering workflows.

This expansion required a corresponding evolution in the system's architecture, particularly in areas such as intent interpretation, contextual understanding, knowledge handling, task decomposition, tool utilization, process management, and computational resource management.

Consequently, Watcher Eye evolved from a domain-oriented analytical system into a broader AI-driven engineering platform.

6. Computational Resource Management and the Development of Nexa

The expansion of Watcher Eye introduced an additional engineering challenge: the computational cost associated with increasingly complex AI and analytical workloads.

A practical engineering system cannot assume unlimited computational resources. It must be capable of operating across heterogeneous hardware environments, including systems with constrained memory and processing capabilities.

This requirement led to the development of Nexa, a resource-management component designed to monitor computational conditions during intensive processing.

Nexa monitors system resources, including RAM utilization and CPU conditions, while also observing the state of active computational processes.

When available memory approaches critical limits or processor conditions become unsuitable for continued processing, Nexa can intervene by temporarily suspending the active computational operation while preserving the latest available processing state or cache.

When system resources return to an acceptable operating condition, the process can subsequently resume from its preserved state rather than restarting the entire computation.

This approach introduces a resource-aware execution model in which computational processes are not treated as independent of their physical execution environment. Instead, the system continuously considers the relationship between AI computation, system resources, process state, and hardware conditions.

7. Research Direction

The overall development trajectory from SCADA systems to embedded platforms, computer vision, automotive ECU technologies, specialized engineering software, and ultimately AI-based engineering systems represents a continuous accumulation of practical engineering knowledge.

The progression can therefore be viewed as a sequence:

Industrial Control → Embedded Systems → IoT → Computer Vision → Automotive ECU Engineering → Binary and Pattern Analysis → Engineering Software → AI-Assisted Engineering → Resource-Aware AI Systems

Within this progression, Watcher Eye represents a transition from conventional task-specific engineering software toward a more general computational architecture capable of assisting engineers in analyzing complex technical environments.

Its development is guided by the principle that artificial intelligence should function not merely as a conversational interface, but as an engineering intelligence layer capable of interpreting objectives, analyzing technical information, interacting with computational tools, managing processes, identifying patterns, and supporting engineering decision-making.

At the same time, the architecture maintains the principle of Human-in-the-Loop Engineering, recognizing that AI-generated analysis and recommendations should remain subject to engineering validation and human judgment, particularly in critical or safety-sensitive environments.

8. Closing Perspective

The research and development work of Dr. Eng. Sayed Mostafa therefore represents an evolutionary engineering path rather than a collection of independent projects. Each stage contributed technical knowledge that enabled the next: industrial control established experience with real-world systems; embedded and IoT development strengthened hardware–software integration; computer vision introduced intelligent data interpretation; automotive engineering introduced complex binary and control-system analysis; and the development of specialized software provided the foundation for increasingly autonomous computational reasoning.

Within this context, Watcher Eye represents the culmination of an extended development process aimed at transforming accumulated engineering knowledge into an intelligent computational framework.

The broader objective is to explore how artificial intelligence can be integrated into real engineering environments to assist with the analysis, interpretation, monitoring, development, and operation of complex technical systems, while preserving engineering accountability and human oversight.
